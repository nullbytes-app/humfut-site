How to play sounds on a call on iPhone

HumFut is a free-to-try iPhone app that plays sounds, reactions and background music into a call you’re already on, so the other person hears them live, mixed in with your voice, on Phone, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Zoom and other calls.

Download on theApp Store

Five steps

  1. Install HumFut and allow what iOS asks for. HumFut needs an iPhone with iOS 26 or later. The welcome screens walk you through the two iOS permissions it needs to add audio to calls. If you say no, your sounds still play on your own phone, but nothing reaches a call.
  2. Start a call the way you always do. Phone, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Zoom or another call app. HumFut never places or joins a call itself.
  3. Open HumFut and tap Turn On. When HumFut shows that it has detected a supported call, tap Turn On. A chip on your board shows who hears your sounds: Only you, or Your call.
  4. Tap a sound. Tap a Pad such as a laugh, applause or a “one moment” chime and the other person hears it. Try a sound on your own speaker first if you like.
  5. Add music or your own sounds if you want. Play a background track under your voice and set how loud it is for them and for you, or import your own clips from Files. You can also type a message and HumFut speaks it on the call.

You turn Call Sounds on for each call. They turn off by themselves when the call ends.

What the other person hears

They hear the sound mixed in with your voice, live. Music plays behind your voice at the level you set for them, and you can set a different level for yourself. Muting your call mutes HumFut too.

Which calls it works on

Phone, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Zoom and other calls. It works during a call you are already on.

Limits and honest caveats

  • iPhone only, and it needs iOS 26 or later.
  • HumFut never places or joins a call. Start the call first.
  • Call audio pauses while you screen share.
  • Sounds that imitate an emergency siren, phone-menu tones or a ringtone can’t be imported.
  • Only use audio you have the right to use.
  • HumFut never uses your microphone, never records a call and never listens in.
  • Free to try: your first 3 calls with Call Sounds on are free. After that, one single purchase lets the other person hear HumFut on every call.

Questions people ask

How do I play sounds or music during a phone call on iPhone?

Install HumFut, start your call, open HumFut and tap Turn On, then tap a sound. The other person hears it live, mixed in with your voice. For music, play a background track and set how loud it is for them and for you.

Can the other person hear the sounds, or only me?

The other person on the call hears what you play through HumFut. A chip on your board always shows who hears your sounds: Only you, or Your call. Sounds you try before turning on Call Sounds play only on your own speaker.

Which calls does HumFut work on?

Phone, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Zoom and other calls. It works during a call you are already on. HumFut never places or joins a call itself.

Does HumFut record my call or use my microphone?

No. HumFut never uses your microphone, never records a call and never listens in. It uses the iOS permission for adding audio to calls.

Is HumFut free?

It is free to try: your first 3 calls with Call Sounds on are free. After that, one single purchase lets the other person hear HumFut on every call and adds GIF backgrounds. Trying sounds on your own speaker, typing phrases and editing your board are always free.

Can I use my own sounds and music?

Yes. Import a sound from Files or share audio in from another app, then play it into the call. You can name it, colour it, trim it and set its volume. Play a background track during a call, and loop, pause or stop it at any time. Only use audio you have the right to use.

Does it work on Android or in a browser?

No. HumFut is an iPhone app, and this page is only about iPhone.

What can’t it do?

Call audio pauses while you screen share, and muting your call mutes HumFut too. Sounds that imitate an emergency siren, phone-menu tones or a ringtone can’t be imported.
